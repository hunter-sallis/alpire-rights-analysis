What happens next

  1. Confirm your society — and join one if you have not

    This is the first step, because it governs everything else. We searched BMI and ASCAP and found no registration in your name, so either you are affiliated with a society outside the United States, or you are not affiliated anywhere yet. Tell us which, and if you already have a society, send us your IPI number — it is the number that identifies you as a writer everywhere in the world.

    If you are not affiliated yet, ASCAP is the cheaper way in. As a new member, joining as Writer & Publisher together is free. Applying as a writer alone is also free, but adding the publisher side later costs $50 — so do both in the same application. This matters more than the $50: performance royalties are split into a writer share and a publisher share, and without a publisher account the publisher half of your own songs has nowhere to go. BMI charges a one-time application fee for writers and a larger one for publishers, shown at the final step of their application; check their current amounts before deciding.

    ASCAP — Writer & Publisher · free BMI — songwriter application BMI — publisher application

    On the ASCAP form choose New Member, then the Writer & Publisher option — that is the one that is free. Selecting Writer alone is also free, but you would then pay $50 to add the publisher side later.

    Two things to know before you apply. You can only belong to one U.S. performing rights organisation at a time — if you are already with a society, switching requires a release letter from your current one before you can join another. And if you are already affiliated abroad, joining a U.S. society is a real decision rather than a formality; talk to us first so we do not create a second identity that competes with the one you already have.

  2. Verify the splits on your priority songs

    Starting with Que Comience La Fiesta, then Takate, Almighty Aletoso and Flauta. For each one we need whatever exists in writing — a split sheet, a producer agreement, a beat licence, or even a text or DM where the split was agreed. Anything in writing helps. The people whose paperwork unlocks these: Davinson Loaiza and Alexis Page on Que Comience La Fiesta, Joel Garcia, Alejandro Mosqueda Paz and Junior Medina on the others.

  3. Resolve the 25 HORAS record

    We need to work out whether your track is a version of the Proyecto Uno composition and, if it is, whether your credit reflects new material you added. This is a reconciliation against works that are already registered and claimed by other people, so it gets handled separately from ordinary registration work and more carefully.

  4. Fix the metadata at your distributor

    Replace the placeholder and nickname composer strings with legal names and IPI numbers, so that everything you release from here on goes out with writer data a registry can actually match. This costs nothing and prevents the same problem recurring on every future release.

  5. Register, then attach your recordings

    Once your identity is confirmed and the splits are documented, we register the compositions — at your society first, since nothing is registered anywhere we can see — and then attach the recordings, including both ISRCs on Que Comience La Fiesta. A song only moves to ready-to-file when its evidence is complete. We will never file a claim on your behalf without documentation; that protects you as much as it protects us.
